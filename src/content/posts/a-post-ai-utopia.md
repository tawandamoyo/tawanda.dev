Lately I have been thinking much about work, money, and economics, and how they might all be irrevocably altered by Artificial Intelligence.

This is just a stream of consciousness post of things I have been loosely thinking about. I am sure I will at some gather them into something more coherent. 

- Will artificial intelligence eliminate the need to work?
- Will AI usher in a new age where there's no need to work? 

Aristotle says, in the Politics:

> We can imagine a situation in which each instrument could do its own work, at the word of command or by intelligent anticipation, like the statues of Daedalus or the tripods made by Hephaestus, of which the poet relates that Of their own motion they entered the conclave of Gods on Olympus.* A shuttle would then weave of itself, and a plectrum would do its own harp-playing. In this situation managers would not need subordinates and masters would not need slaves.

&mdash; Aristotle, <cite>Nichomachean Ethics</cite>

- What will such an age look like, where man does not need to work? 
- Will it be an age of unending abundance, and leisure that leads to an explosion of creativity? 

- What will become of the human psyche when everything is done by machines. Clearly the increasing fusion of human and machine is changing us in many ways not many of them good. 

There is a story in the Chuang Zi of a man who rejects the use of tools, saying that where there are machines there are bound to be machine worries, and when there are machine worries the human gets a "machine heart", and something human within man dies.
